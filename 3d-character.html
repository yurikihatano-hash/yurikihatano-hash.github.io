01 / Overview

An angelic
figure in motion.

For KNB205, I developed a stylised character whose look draws on angelic imagery, especially the Seraphim, and took it from sculpt through final presentation.

The production moved between ZBrush, Maya and Substance 3D Painter: sculpting, rebuilding topology, unwrapping UVs, designing materials, creating a HumanIK rig and posing the finished character for rendered images.

02 / Production workflow

A connected
pipeline.

The character's asymmetrical sculpt, graphic clothing and oversized silhouette made the transitions between modelling, materials and rigging especially important.

01 / Sculpt, topology and UVs

Building a
usable mesh.

After sculpting in ZBrush, I rebuilt the asymmetrical character in Maya. The eyes, ears, hands and spaces between the fingers required particularly careful retopology. I reduced the heavy sculpt with ZBrush Decimation Master to keep the Maya workflow manageable, cleaned up n-gons and non-manifold geometry, then planned flatter UV islands for the graphics on the T-shirt.

03 / Materials and visual identity

Details with
a purpose.

In Substance 3D Painter, I layered skin tones and subtle surface variation rather than using one flat colour. The white hair was given a reflective gold treatment to reinforce the angelic character concept.

The T-shirt includes YHWH lettering, a cross, stars and galaxy imagery. Time constraints ruled out separate wings, so wing-inspired motifs carried the Seraphim reference onto the arms and legs. For the eyes, I adapted a base texture from Adobe Community Assets.

Back in Maya, I connected the separate texture sets to the relevant Arnold Standard Surface shaders to prepare the character for rendering.

04 / Rigging and expression

Posed for
the frame.

I built a HumanIK skeleton and created facial blend shapes for different expressions. Keeping every duplicated expression mesh in the Maya scene made the file too heavy, so I changed the workflow: add each target to the blend shape, archive it separately, then remove the duplicate from the working file.

The oversized shirt complicated shoulder-joint placement. I prioritised the silhouette for the final renders and refined skin weights where they visibly affected the poses. The result is presented as a posed character study, rather than as a claim of a production-ready animation rig.
