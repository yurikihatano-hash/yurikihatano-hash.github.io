A story told through an empty seat, a field of swords and a room reclaimed by time.

01 / Overview

A world after
its ruler.

The Forgotten Ruler’s Throne is a real-time 3D environment film I created for a university project in late October 2025.

I modelled meshes for the virtual environment in Autodesk Maya 2026, developed textures and materials in Adobe Substance 3D Painter, then assembled and presented the scene in Unreal Engine 5.6.

02 / The story

A throne no one
can claim.

The hall belonged to a ruler whose kingdom has disappeared from both history and maps. When rebels from across the world stormed the chamber, the king and his knights survived the battle, but the destruction changed him.

He renounced his authority and drove his sword into the throne so that no one could sit there again. His knights planted their own swords in the floor as a sign of respect. Two stone monuments were left to preserve their oath; the chamber was sealed. Over the centuries, the world forgot the place entirely.

03 / Design intent

History written
into the room.

The design had two jobs: to build a ceremonial great hall within a cave-like setting, and to make the violence of the battle and the passage of time visible without explaining them through dialogue.

Repeated swords point toward the throne; damaged metal, blood-stained surfaces, rust, moss and overgrowth suggest both conflict and abandonment. The long red carpet and monumental architecture give the space the weight of a former seat of power.

Visual reference / One Piece

The Room of Flowers and the Empty Throne at Mary Geoise helped me think about how a room or object can carry a story before a character appears in it. I used those spaces as references for the relationship between power, symbolism and environment design.

04 / Asset breakdown

Building the
evidence.

The throne, swords and inscribed stones are the objects that make the backstory legible in the environment.

01 / The throne

Power left
behind.

The focal asset combines the throne, its raised base, a pair of orbs and a red carpet. The king’s sword pierces the seat, turning the symbol of authority into a refusal to let another ruler take his place.

02 / The swords

Many blades.
One battle.

I built several sword variations from a common base: the king’s sword in the throne and the knights’ swords along the carpet. Some remain intact; others are broken, blood-stained, rusted or discoloured by plants and mould. Those differences suggest the force of the battle and the years that followed it.

03 / The stone monuments

A vow made
permanent.

Two inscribed monuments preserve the king’s oath and the agreement made in the chamber. Inspired by the Poneglyphs in One Piece, the stones gave me a technical challenge: how to apply writing convincingly to a 3D surface. I learned the text treatment through a YouTube tutorial, then integrated the inscriptions into the environment.
